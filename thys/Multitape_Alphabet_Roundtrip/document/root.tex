\title{Round-Trip Composition of the Alphabet Transformations\\[6pt]
       \large Formal Proof in Isabelle/HOL}
\author{Andr\'as Z.~Salamon \and Michael Wehar}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Machine-checked formal proof in Isabelle/HOL that the two multitape
tape-alphabet transformations compose \emph{as machines}, in both orders,
with the accepted language preserved and the composed running time bounded
by an \emph{explicit} affine function of the source machine's running time.

The two transformations are alphabet \emph{enlargement} ($\enl$) and
alphabet \emph{reduction} ($\red$).  Enlargement is the
Hartmanis--Stearns / Hopcroft--Ullman linear-speedup
construction~\cite{Hartmanis1965:computational,Hopcroft1979:introduction},
which packs $c$ contiguous tape cells into one block to run a machine
$c$ times faster.  Reduction is the elementary per-symbol binary
re-encoding of an arbitrary finite tape alphabet onto the fixed
four-symbol alphabet $\mathrm{sym4}$ --- the folklore alphabet
homomorphism~\cite[p.~23]{Balcazar1988:structural}, set within the wider
machine-simulation landscape by van Emde Boas and
Papadimitriou~\cite{vanEmdeBoas1990:machine,Papadimitriou1994:computational}.
They are developed independently in the sibling sessions \sessAE{} and
\sessAR{} over the shared nondeterministic multitape substrate
\sessMTTM.  This session (\sessART) composes them.

For a well-formed source $M$ with $|\Gamma_M| \geq 4$ and each order, the
session establishes:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Language preservation}, an equivalence modulo the composed
  input re-encoding
  (\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_enlarge\_language} and
  \texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_reduce\_language});
\item a \emph{composed linear-time bound with explicit constants}
  (\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_enlarge\_time\_explicit} and
  \texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_reduce\_time\_explicit}).  Writing $k$ for the
  source tape count and $b = \lceil \log_2 |\Gamma_M| \rceil$ for its
  per-symbol block width, reducing then enlarging runs in time
  $8(6k + 1)\,b\,T(|w|) + 2\,b\,|w| + 4$, and enlarging then reducing in
  $8(6k + 1)\,b'\,T(|w| + c - 1) + 2(6k + 1)\,b'\,|w| + 4(6k + 1)\,b'$, where
  $c$ is the block size and $b'$ the block width of the enlarged alphabet.
  Each bound is also given, as a same-named corollary, in the existential
  shape $\exists\, A, B, C.\ \mathrm{time} \leq A\,T(|w|) + B\,|w| + C$ ---
  these composed constants are established here, not inherited from a prior
  result.
\end{itemize}

Nothing is re-proved: each headline chains the two sibling sessions'
existing language-preservation and explicit-time theorems
(\texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_time\_explicit} and
\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_time\_explicit}).
The composed constants
expose an order asymmetry invisible under $O(\cdot)$: the enlargement's
block-speedup factor $c$ \emph{cancels} when the alphabet is reduced first
but \emph{survives} when it is enlarged first.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview}

The substrate \sessMTTM{} is nondeterministic by default: a machine's
transition is a relation, and $\mathrm{accepts\_in\_time\_mttm}\,
M\, w\, t$ means \emph{some} run on $w$ reaches the accept state within $t$
steps.  A tape-alphabet transformation is a total function on machines;
each one changes only the alphabet and copies the tape count.  The two
transformations run opposite ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item $M_e = \enl M$ groups $c = |{'c}|$ contiguous cells into one
  block, so its tape alphabet is the block alphabet
  $\mathrm{gamma\_block}\,\Gamma_M$ (all functions ${'c} \to \Gamma_M$, of
  cardinality $|\Gamma_M|^{\,c}$) and it simulates $M$ with a
  factor-$c$ speedup.  Its input is re-encoded by
  $\mathrm{encode\_input}\, bl_M\, w$, of length $\lceil |w| / c \rceil$.
\item $M_r = \red M$ spells each source symbol as a fixed-width block of
  $\mathrm{sym4} = \{\mathrm{BLANK4}, \mathrm{LE4}, \mathrm{BIT0},
  \mathrm{BIT1}\}$ cells and simulates $M$ with a slowdown logarithmic in
  $|\Gamma_M|$.  Its input is re-encoded by
  $\mathrm{encode\_input\_ar}\, \Gamma_M\, bl_M\, w$, of length exactly
  $b \cdot |w|$, where
  $b = \max(1, \lceil \log_2 |\Gamma_M| \rceil)$ is the per-symbol
  encoding width.
\end{itemize}

Because a composed term such as $\red(\enl M)$ names constants from
\emph{both} sibling sessions, it cannot be stated inside either without a
cross-import cycle (the sessions are deliberately independent:
\sessAE{} and \sessAR{} never import each other).  \sessART{} is a new
session whose parent is \sessMTTM{} and whose \texttt{ROOT} lists both
siblings, giving the composition an honest home above both.

This round trip (applying enlargement and reduction to the same
machine, in sequence) does not appear in the classical literature, where
the two constructions are presented separately.  It is at once a minimal
worked example of using both transformations together and, through the
explicit composed constants, a concrete illustration of how the
\emph{order} of two linear-time transformations changes the constant that
$O(\cdot)$ would hide.

\paragraph{Terminology.}
The names \emph{alphabet enlargement} and \emph{alphabet reduction} are
ours, chosen to make the duality explicit: one grows the tape alphabet
to buy speed, the other shrinks it to a fixed size.  In the classical
literature the two carry different names and are not usually paired.
Enlargement is the alphabet-growing half of the Hartmanis--Stearns
tape-compression / linear-speedup
theorem~\cite{Hartmanis1965:computational}, given a textbook proof by
Hopcroft and Ullman~\cite[Theorems 12.3--12.4]{Hopcroft1979:introduction}.
Reduction is the folklore normalisation that a multitape machine over
$\Gamma$ is simulated over a fixed small alphabet at a per-machine
constant-factor cost of $\lceil \log_2 |\Gamma| \rceil$ cells per
symbol (a \emph{fixed} factor, not a $\log T$ slowdown; that slowdown
belongs to the separate tape-count reduction); it is the alphabet
homomorphism onto a two-or-more-symbol alphabet stated by Balc\'azar,
D\'iaz, and Gabarr\'o~\cite[p.~23]{Balcazar1988:structural}, and sits
within the general machine-model-simulation landscape surveyed by van Emde
Boas~\cite{vanEmdeBoas1990:machine} and
Papadimitriou~\cite{Papadimitriou1994:computational}.  Classic texts
\emph{assume} the reduction rather than cost it.  Hopcroft and
Ullman~\cite[\S7.2]{Hopcroft1969:formal} invoke it only to justify a
universal machine over a fixed alphabet, with no time analysis.  The
explicit constants below make visible a factor the classical
presentations leave implicit.

\paragraph{Related AFP developments.}
The round trip itself --- applying both alphabet transformations to one
machine --- has no formalised precedent; it composes the two sibling
entries.  Among the neighbouring Isabelle/HOL developments, Balbach's
\texttt{Cook\_Levin}~\cite{Balbach2023:Cook} is the closest in spirit, since
it too composes verified machine transformations (and its
\texttt{Two\_Four\_Symbols} encoding is the closest prior to the reduction
half), but it neither enlarges an alphabet for speed nor tracks the composed
constants that reordering the two transformations exposes.  Xu, Zhang,
Urban, and Joosten's
\texttt{Universal\_Turing\_Machine}~\cite{Xu2019:universal} works over a
fixed binary alphabet, and Dalvit and Thiemann's
\texttt{Multitape\_To\_Singletape\_TM}~\cite{Dalvit2022:verified} --- the
substrate's model ancestor --- fixes the tape count in the machine's type,
whereas our substrate makes it a value, so the round trip is stated once for
machines of every tape count.

\section{The round-trip theorems}

Throughout, $M$ is a source machine with $\mathrm{well\_formed\_mttm}\, M$
and $|\Gamma_M| \geq 4$; $k = k_{\mathrm{tm}}\, M$ is its tape count;
$c = |{'c}|$ is the enlargement block size; and
$b = \max(1, \lceil \log_2 |\Gamma_M| \rceil)$.  Both combinators
preserve the tape count (\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_preserves\_tape\_count}
and \texttt{k\_tm\_alphabet\_enlarge}), so a single $k$ threads the
whole composition.

\subsection{Language preservation}

Each direction chains the two sibling equivalences, discharging one seam
locally: the reduced machine is well-formed for any valid four-symbol
source (\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_well\_formed}), and the enlarged
machine's four-symbol lower bound follows from the constant-cell embedding
of the source alphabet into the block alphabet
(\texttt{card\_gamma\_block\_ge\_card}).

\paragraph{Reduce, then enlarge.}
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    &\mathrm{encode\_input}\, bl_{M_r}\, \\
    &\qquad (\mathrm{encode\_input\_ar}\, \Gamma_M\, bl_M\, w)
      \in L\bigl(\enl(\red M)\bigr) \\
    &\qquad\qquad \Longleftrightarrow\ w \in L(M)
  \end{aligned}
\]
(\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_enlarge\_language}).

\paragraph{Enlarge, then reduce.}
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    &\mathrm{encode\_input\_ar}\, \Gamma_{M_e}\, bl_{M_e}\, \\
    &\qquad (\mathrm{encode\_input}\, bl_M\, w)
      \in L\bigl(\red(\enl M)\bigr) \\
    &\qquad\qquad \Longleftrightarrow\ w \in L(M)
  \end{aligned}
\]
(\texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_reduce\_language}).

\subsection{Composed time bounds, with explicit constants}

The composed slowdown is obtained by routing the \emph{inner} machine's
running-time bound in as the \emph{outer} transformation's per-input time
function.  Making the base bounds explicit makes the composed constants
explicit too.  The base bounds are: enlargement in
$2\lceil |w|/c \rceil + 8\lceil T(|w|)/c \rceil + 4$
(\texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_time\_explicit}, constants independent of the
machine), and reduction in $(6k + 1)\, b\, T(|w|)$
(\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_time\_explicit}, with linear and additive terms
zero).

\paragraph{Reduce, then enlarge
(\texttt{alphabet\_reduce\_enlarge\_time\_explicit}).}
If $M$ accepts $w$ within $T(|w|)$ steps, then $\enl(\red M)$ accepts the
composed encoding within
\[
  8\,(6k + 1)\, b\, T(|w|)
  \;+\; 2\, b\, |w| \;+\; 4
\]
steps.  The enlargement's block factor $c$ \emph{cancels}: it divides both
the intermediate length and the routed running time, but the ceiling
divisions are discarded in the relaxation to the affine form, so $c$ does
not appear.  Reducing first fixes the alphabet at four symbols; the
subsequent block speedup then buys nothing on the dominant term.

\paragraph{Enlarge, then reduce
(\texttt{alphabet\_enlarge\_reduce\_time\_explicit}, assuming
$\mathrm{mono}\, T$).}
Writing $D = 6k + 1$ and
$b' = \max(1, \lceil c \cdot \log_2 |\Gamma_M| \rceil)$ (the
per-symbol width of the \emph{block} alphabet
$\mathrm{gamma\_block}\, \Gamma_M$, of cardinality $|\Gamma_M|^{\,c}$): if
$M$ accepts $w$ within $T(|w|)$ steps, then $\red(\enl M)$ accepts the
composed encoding within
\[
  8\, D\, b'\, T(|w| + c - 1)
  \;+\; 2\, D\, b'\, |w|
  \;+\; 4\, D\, b'
\]
steps.  Here $c$ \emph{does not} cancel.  Enlarging first inflates the
alphabet to $|\Gamma_M|^{\,c}$, so the reduction must spend
$b' \approx c \cdot \log_2 |\Gamma_M|$ cells per symbol, exactly
undoing the factor-$c$ speedup on the dominant term, and $c$ survives
both in the time argument $T(|w| + c - 1)$ (the intermediate length is the
lossy $\lceil |w|/c \rceil$, so $\mathrm{mono}\, T$ and a rounding to the
next block boundary are needed) and, through $b'$, in every coefficient.

\paragraph{The asymmetry.}
The two orders' leading $T$-coefficients are $8(6k + 1)\, b$ for
reduce-then-enlarge and $8(6k + 1)\, b'$ for enlarge-then-reduce.  Reducing
first leaves this coefficient \emph{independent of the block size} $c$: the
alphabet is already fixed at four symbols, so the enlargement's factor-$c$
speedup is discarded in the relaxation to the affine form and $c$ drops out.
Enlarging first instead inflates the alphabet to $|\Gamma_M|^{\,c}$, so its
block width $b' = \lceil c \log_2 |\Gamma_M| \rceil \approx c\, b$ carries a
factor of $c$ into every coefficient, and the leading term grows to
$\approx c \cdot 8(6k + 1)\, b$.  The same two transformations, composed in
opposite orders, thus differ by a factor of about $c$ in the leading
constant --- under $O(\cdot)$ both are simply ``linear time'' and the
distinction is lost; the explicit constants recover it.  These are upper
bounds; matching lower bounds are future work (they require a
minimum-displacement argument the one-directional simulation lemmas do not
provide).
