\title{A deep embedding of HOL in HOL:\\ soundness, completeness, consistency}
\author{Christoph Benzm\"uller \and Daniel Kirchner}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
This entry presents a machine-checked deep embedding of classical
higher-order logic (HOL) in Isabelle/HOL, following Benzm\"uller, Brown
and Kohlhase (BKK)~\cite{BBK04}.  Throughout, HOL means Church's simple
theory of types~\cite{Church40}: it is the embedded \emph{object} logic,
while Isabelle/HOL serves as the ambient meta-logic.  The language of
BKK~\S2 is formalised in a \emph{locally nameless} representation, which
makes BKK's identification of $\alpha$-equivalent terms a literal
equality, so that no $\alpha$-conversion or bound-variable renaming is
needed.  The semantics of BKK~\S3 is rendered abstractly, up to the
class $\mathcal{M}_{\beta\mathfrak{f}\mathfrak{b}}$ of $\Sigma$-Henkin
models; standard models arise as the full special case.  The signature
includes BKK's optional primitive equality (Remark~7.9) and---following
Andrews~\cite{Andrews72}, beyond BKK---typed description operators.

On this basis the natural-deduction calculus $\mathrm{NK}$ of BKK~\S7 is
proved sound (Theorem~7.3) and Henkin-complete, via the model-existence
method of BKK~\S6, with the term model realised as a term evaluation.
Completeness holds in a form stronger than BKK's Corollary~7.7: at every
infinite value carrier, for every signature with infinitely many
parameters, and for open formulas.  It also holds relative to
hypotheses.  For derivability from hypotheses in Andrews' sense---some
finite part of the hypothesis set (the \emph{context}) derives the
conclusion---any context of open formulas is admitted, over any infinite
signature, once the carrier is at least as large as the signature;
semantic compactness of the Henkin consequence follows.  For the
calculus itself, a finite context needs nothing beyond well-formedness,
and over a countable signature a context of open formulas that leaves
infinitely many parameters unused is admitted at every infinite
carrier.  Consistency---that $\bot$ is not derivable---follows from a
concrete $\Sigma$-standard model with finite domains: a single
individual and finitely many non-logical constants suffice, so no axiom
of infinity is assumed for the embedded logic.  Model existence is
likewise concrete: from the consistency of a sentence over a countable
signature, a Henkin (general) model of it with countable total domain
is defined in plain HOL---non-effectively, via a maximal-consistent
extension, but with consistency as the only premise.

As an illustration, Cantor's theorem is derived \emph{inside}
$\mathrm{NK}$, in surjective and injective form and at every
type~\cite{SEPChurchTT}.  Finally, two syntactic renderings of infinity
are compared with each other and with semantic infinitude.  The
constant-free first-order sentences ``there are at least $n$
individuals''---the axiomatisation of ``the domain is infinite'' in the
language of equality---are consistent with $\mathrm{NK}$, by
compactness over arbitrarily large finite models: the consistency proof
constructs no infinite model.  The single Dedekind-style axiom of
infinity derives each of these sentences, but they do not derive the
axiom: no finite part of them does, and a Henkin model of them all,
countable over a countable signature, refutes it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{What is new.}
Concurrently and independently, From, Huerta y Munive and
Schlichtkrull~\cite{Q0CompletenessAFP} mechanised completeness of
Andrews' $\mathcal{Q}_0$ for general models; their development builds on
D\'iaz's \emph{Metatheory of $\mathcal{Q}_0$}~\cite{Q0MetatheoryAFP} and
thereby on set theory axiomatised in HOL (\texttt{ZFC\_in\_HOL}).  The
present entry instead embeds the object logic and constructs its models
in plain Isabelle/HOL, with no set-theoretic universe---which is what
we mean by HOL \emph{in} HOL.  To the best of our knowledge it is the
first machine-checked completeness proof for a deep embedding of HOL over
plain HOL; Harrison's self-verification of HOL Light~\cite{Harrison06}
concerns soundness and consistency, not completeness, and the nearest
completeness results treat weaker logics (second-order logic in
Coq~\cite{KochKirst22}; a synthetic framework for propositional,
first-order, modal and hybrid logic~\cite{From25}).

The entry formalises BKK's model-existence method (BKK~\S6)---the
saturated maximal-consistent extension and its closure ($\nabla$)
properties---for the Henkin class
$\mathcal{M}_{\beta\mathfrak{f}\mathfrak{b}}$, and \emph{extends} BKK by
typed description operators after Andrews~\cite{Andrews72} (the rule
$NK(\iota)$ and its model condition, carried through soundness, model
existence and completeness).  Completeness holds in three forms.  For
\emph{valid} formulas it is stronger than BKK's Corollary~7.7: for open
formulas, at every infinite carrier and for every signature with
infinitely many parameters, with no cardinality link between the two.
Relative to hypotheses, $\Phi \models A \Rightarrow \Phi \vdash A$ holds
for finite contexts of open formulas without further conditions, and for
infinite contexts of open formulas that leave a parameter reserve as
large as the signature; a transfinite extension lemma takes this to
BKK's arbitrary signature cardinalities $\aleph_s$ (Remark~3.16,
arbitrary within the type universes of the meta-logic), where the
carrier must be at least as large as the signature.  For derivability
from hypotheses in Andrews' sense (some finite sub-context derives the
conclusion) the reserve condition disappears, and semantic compactness
of the Henkin consequence is obtained as a corollary, with no
ultraproducts.  Throughout, free variables are replaced by fresh
parameters simultaneously, by a substitution-value law for the whole
context at once.

\paragraph{Consistency: two tiers.}
Two further points concern \emph{consistency} rather than completeness.
The first stays in plain HOL.  By compactness over arbitrarily large
\emph{finite} models, $\mathrm{NK}$ is consistent with the constant-free
first-order sentences ``there are at least $n$ individuals'', and with
the scheme of inequations $c_i \neq c_j$ between new individual
constants through which the formalisation obtains this---the classical
device of the compactness argument by expansion with new constants
(Skolem~\cite{Skolem34}, Mal'cev~\cite{Malcev36},
Henkin~\cite{Henkin49,Henkin50}, Robinson~\cite{Robinson63}), which
Andrews' \S55~\cite{Andrews02} (Theorem 5506) applies in type theory to
obtain nonstandard models; the consistency proof uses no infinite model
and no strengthening of the meta-theory.  The sentences are compared
with the single Dedekind-style axiom of infinity: the axiom derives, for
every $n$, the sentence that there are at least $n$ individuals, but
neither these sentences nor the scheme derive the axiom, and a Henkin
model of them all refutes it.

The second tier concerns $\mathrm{NK}$ with the Dedekind-style axiom of
infinity itself.  Such an axiom has no finite models and so falls outside the plain-HOL setting of
this entry; it is handled in the companion entry
\texttt{HOL\_in\_HOL\_Deep\_Infinity\_ZFC}~\cite{InfinityZFC} by a
standard model over Paulson's axiomatic set-theoretic universe
\texttt{ZFC\_in\_HOL}~\cite{ZFCinHOL}, at the cost of a stronger,
non-conservative meta-theory and hence with only \emph{relative} (to
$\mathrm{ZF}$) consistency.  That model has full function spaces, so it
also validates the relational axiom of choice, and the same route yields
the relative consistency of full classical higher-order logic (the
calculus $\mathrm{NK}$, which already has extensionality and typed
description, extended by the infinity axiom and choice).  The companion
isolates the universe behind an abstract interface, abstracted further
to a Harrison-style axiom, with infinity as the only surplus: choice
holds in the same full model, using only the meta-level choice of
Isabelle/HOL.

Writing \emph{M-HOL} for the meta-logic Isabelle/HOL and \emph{O-HOL}
for the embedded object logic, the two tiers are
$\text{M-HOL}\vdash\mathrm{con}(\text{O-HOL})$, established here
\emph{outright}, that is, relative only to plain HOL, and
$\text{M-HOL}+V\vdash\mathrm{con}(\text{O-HOL}+\text{infinity}+\text{choice})$,
established in the companion, where $V$ is the axiomatised set-theoretic
universe of \texttt{ZFC\_in\_HOL}.  The first does not conflict with
G\"odel's second theorem: O-HOL has finite models and interprets no
arithmetic.  For the second, some strengthening of the meta-theory is
necessary, since O-HOL with infinity and choice interprets the M-HOL
core and shares its proof-theoretic strength; the companion's
introduction gives this metatheoretic argument, with the references to
Mathias~\cite{Mathias01} and Kun\v{c}ar and Popescu~\cite{KuncarPopescu19}.
Neither tier is the object logic proving its own consistency (cf.\
Harrison's self-verification of HOL Light~\cite{Harrison06}); it is
always M-HOL, one level up.  For many applications the first tier
suffices: where HOL serves as a representation language, as in the
LogiKEy methodology for normative reasoning~\cite{LogiKEy}, no
object-level axiom of infinity is assumed, arithmetic at the object
level is optional, and the monomorphic core of the logic in use (TH0
without the choice operator) is the one certified outright.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the initial release.}
This update is additive.  The headline interface of the initial release
(August 2026) is preserved: the theory names, the exported theorems of
\texttt{Main\_Results} with their statements, and the Cantor results
under their names of the initial release, \texttt{cantor\_surjective} and
\texttt{cantor\_injective}, now aliases of the theorems in theory
\texttt{Cantor}; further lemmas of the initial release that the generalised proofs
no longer need retain their statements and are marked as
compatibility exports in place.  All new material is added on top: the
strengthened completeness ladder, the finitary consequence relation,
the derived rules, the theories \texttt{NK\_Infinity} and
\texttt{Footprint\_Checks}.  Two deliberate exceptions, flagged for
maintainers.  First, the theorem \texttt{completeness} is now BKK's
Corollary~7.7 proper: its premise is consequence from the context
rather than validity (a caller holding a validity proof adapts by one
step), its purity condition is \texttt{richp}, which over countable
signatures is equivalent to the earlier \texttt{freep}
(\texttt{richp\_iff\_freep}), and the countability constraint on the
signature is dropped.  Second, internal auxiliaries superseded by the
generalised proofs were retired, and two internal names
changed.\footnote{Retired: the per-variable induction auxiliaries
\texttt{completeness\_open\_aux} and \texttt{completeness\_open\_any\_aux};
\texttt{freep\_ext}, \texttt{ext\_mono'}, \texttt{fp0},
\texttt{leib\_sym'}, \texttt{leib\_trans'}; the hand-built logical
constants \texttt{cNg}, \texttt{cDs}, \texttt{cPi}, \texttt{cEv},
\texttt{cIv}, \texttt{cIv\_desc} of the concrete model, superseded by the
locale \texttt{lambda\_universe}; \texttt{LamN}; \texttt{Ap\_dom} of
the Hintikka model.  Renamed: the evaluation \texttt{Ev} of the locale
\texttt{valuation} to \texttt{Eval}, and the datatype \texttt{mval} of
the concrete model to \texttt{ival}.}
